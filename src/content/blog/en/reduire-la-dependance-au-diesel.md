In its October 2026 report, the International Energy Agency expects tension on the diesel market to run into 2027.

The situation isn't down to geopolitical crises alone. In its outlook to 2030, the IEA also describes a **structural transformation of refining**: plant closures in Europe, economic pressure on European sites and growing capacity in Asia.

That doesn't mean a permanent shortage: global capacity is expected to grow faster than demand. But more capacity elsewhere doesn't remove supply vulnerabilities here.

For shippers, the question is simple: **how many litres of diesel does your organisation force you to burn to deliver to your customers?**

## Reduce dependence, transform the plans.

The levers are concrete:

- Pool flows between shippers.
- Improve load factors and reduce empty kilometres.
- Adapt delivery frequencies and constraints.
- Build rail and inland-waterway solutions with carriers.

At Pool4ward, we help shippers and their partners identify these possibilities and turn them into operational transport plans.

**Less diesel needed means less exposure to supply tension — and fewer emissions.**

Mad Max should stay a film. Not become your transport strategy.
